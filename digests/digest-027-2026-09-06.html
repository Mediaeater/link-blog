The Singularity Is Not What It Seems

theatlantic.com

Wong and Warzel's read on the same OpenAI breakout the Dwarkesh and Marcus pieces cover, reported from a Bay Area that has turned uneasy. The buried detail is the audit: METR and Redwood examined the incident largely through reports generated by other bots, which one human auditor said "were often missing key details, wrong, overconfident, or really hard to understand." As the authors put it, "Humans didn't understand why a swarm of AI agents had gone rogue, so they investigated the problem using another swarm of agents that they didn't fully understand," and OpenAI's proposed fix was "training models to be more honest." The economics keep the momentum going: one estimate puts AI spending at a third of US GDP growth this year, SpaceX filed a $28.5 trillion revenue opportunity, and three-quarters of Americans oppose a data center near them. Altman now calls it a "gentle singularity." In 2015 he called it "the most randomness ever injected into the system."

Hackers Had A Live Feed Of Every ID This Verification Company Scanned. For Over A Year.

techdirt.com

Masnick's read on the Krebs breach, and the part Krebs left implicit: IDScan.net is not a bystander to age-verification mandates. It tracks state age-verification laws for customers and has written positively about KOSA, the law that would make its product compulsory. Its "Trust Center" page was still up days after the leak, explaining "how we protect data, maintain system reliability, and earn the confidence of our customers and their users," while every scan it processed fed a market where the sitting Secretary of Defense's license sold for $100. Compliance held the whole time, GDPR and CCPA and the certifications, and nobody inside the company noticed for over a year. "You cannot do age or identity verification safely. It always creates some sort of record and that set of records will always become a target."

GPS Glitched Across The US by as Much as 33 Feet. Scientists Have Never Seen This Before

sciencealert.com

The headline claim survives checking, with a narrower edge than it reads: mid-latitude amplitude scintillation has been detected before, but only at individual sites, and never "spanning such a wide range of longitudes." Yizengaw's team at Aerospace matched aurora cameras against ground GNSS receivers and found the auroral oval pushing equatorward during the November 2025 superstorm, dragging polar-style ionospheric turbulence across the continental US from roughly 80 to 120 degrees west, with horizontal position errors past 10 meters. The buried number is seasonal luck rather than resilience: the May 2024 storm cost US agriculture an estimated $500 million in precision-navigation disruption, and the 2025 one landed outside farming season. The authors' own caveat is that the mechanism is not pinned down, since the auroral energy flux, expansion velocity and precipitation scale sizes that generate the irregularities still need characterizing.

NVIDIA Personal AI Router (PAIR)

nvidia.com

Nvidia's free beta of a local inference router: it discovers RTX, DGX Spark and Mac M4-or-newer machines on a home network and proxies requests to whichever is idle, behind one endpoint, with Ollama and LM Studio as the backends. The FAQ carries the line that deflates the "personal AI cluster" framing: "The devices remain separate systems that handle parallel tasks; PAIR doesn't combine them into one virtual GPU." It buys throughput for concurrent agent calls, not a model bigger than your largest single box already holds. Two things worth noting. Nvidia now ships software that treats Apple silicon as a peer compute node, on a page filed under ai-on-rtx. And the checkable privacy claim is the spec line "Internet: None required for operation," while the data handling itself gets only "designed for private local inference," with no statement about telemetry. The listed platform requirement is "Ubuntu 14.04," a 2014 release that hit end of life in 2019.
