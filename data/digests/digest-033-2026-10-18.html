Online ads weaponised to deliver ‘zero-click’ spyware, researchers say

theguardian.com

Citizen Lab says Israeli ad agency Discover-Tech runs a platform called Controlium that lets clients “mass-profile and deanonymise” internet users through ordinary programmatic ads, then hands selected targets to third-party spyware firms for zero-click infection. Dozens of devices at Al Jazeera were allegedly compromised, and Confiant found the ads served inside government, UN, Nato and ICC networks. Bill Marczak: “There has been a lot of buzz that online ads can be used to identify and automatically hack targets with pinpoint precision, but now for the first time, we have concrete technical evidence showing this process in action.”

Furifont: furigana built into the font

furifont.antfu.me

A Japanese font that shows furigana by itself. The text stays plain: no <ruby> markup, no JavaScript, no plugin. Compiled from Source Han Sans or Serif, a build step reads a dictionary of thousands of words and draws the reading into new glyphs as OpenType ligatures, so 今日 becomes one glyph with きょう above it. The catch is structural: “a font sees one run of kanji at a time, not the sentence around it,” so readings can be wrong.
